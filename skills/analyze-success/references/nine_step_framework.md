# Nine-Step Distillation Framework — Detailed Reference

This file elaborates each step of the nine-step distillation method with examples, diagnostic questions, and common failure modes. The agent should load this reference when deep guidance per step is needed, while the SKILL.md contains the concise specification.

---

## Step 1: Find Multiple Successes

**Core question:** Is the success pattern cross-context, cross-period, and cross-domain repeatable?

**Why it matters:** Single success can be luck, era dividends, or resource advantage. True genius shows repeatability.

**Examples of repeatable vs non-repeatable:**

| Subject | Repeatable? | Evidence |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Deng Xiaoping | Yes | Rural reform, urban reform, cadre reform, opening-up, ideology management, diplomacy — all exhibit similar "incremental breakthrough" capability |
| Grothendieck | Yes | Functional analysis, homological algebra, algebraic geometry, topos theory, motives — repeatedly "reinventing language, unifying structures" |
| Steve Jobs | Yes | Macintosh, Pixar, iPod, iPhone, iPad — repeatedly "reversing from complete user experience" |
| One-hit product manager | No | Single hit feature; no evidence of repeatability across different contexts |

**Minimum evidence threshold:** At least 3 distinct successes in different contexts. If N < 3, flag as "insufficient evidence for generator-level analysis" and proceed only with moves/mechanisms, not the generator layer.

**Common failure mode:** Assuming a single remarkable achievement implies a replicable method.

---

## Step 2: Recover the Original Problem

**Core question:** What dead-end did they face that others couldn't see clearly? How did they redefine the problem differently?

**Key insight:** The greatness is not in the elegant answer, but in seeing a problem that others mis-identified or didn't see at all.

**Translation formula:**

> Others saw the problem as **[X]**. The subject redefined it as **[Y]**.

**Examples:**
- Deng Xiaoping: Redefined the problem from "socialism vs capitalism" to "can productivity be developed"
- Grothendieck: Redefined the problem from "how to prove this theorem" to "is our current language of space too narrow"
- Toyota: Redefined the problem from "how to produce more" to "how to make waste visible and eliminate it at the source"
- Jobs: Redefined the problem from "how to add more features" to "what complete experience would feel inevitable"

**Common failure mode:** Describing what they did (the answer) without reconstructing what problem they perceived. If you can't articulate the original dead-end, you'll inevitably copy the wrong answer.

---

## Step 3: Translate Actions into System Functions

**Core question:** What function did each observable action serve in the system?

**Mandatory translation formula:**

> X is not X; its function is Y.

**Examples:**
- "Special Economic Zones are not preferential zones; their function is institutional experiment sandboxes with low resistance to old rules"
- "Schemes are not abstract nouns; their function is to enlarge the definition of 'space' so that number theory, geometry, local rings, degenerations, and singular phenomena enter the same language"
- "Pragmatism is not a personality trait; its function is switching the evaluation criterion from ideological purity to performance feedback"
- "Kanban is not sticky notes; its function is making inventory waste visible and compressing feedback to the shop floor"
- "Examinations are not selection tools; their function is replacing bloodline gatekeeping with abstract rules"
- "Abstraction is not distance from reality; its function is finding higher-dimensional operational space"
- "Brand is not a logo; its function is compressing trust and aesthetic judgment"

**Common failure mode:** Describing the action and stopping. Always force the "its function is Y" half of the sentence.

---

## Step 4: Find the Energy Converter

**Core question:** What human desire, resource, contradiction, fear, competition, or tension did they convert into constructive driving force?

**Why it matters:** No exceptional system runs on willpower alone. It must tap into an existing energy source and redirect it.

**Examples:**
- Deng Xiaoping: Desire for wealth, local bureaucratic promotion incentives, foreign capital's profit motive, families' desire to improve life
- Grothendieck: The structural tension behind mathematical exceptions, chaos, and repeated similarity — "these should not all be different"
- Steve Jobs: Human craving for complete experience, aesthetic satisfaction, identity expression, and friction-free tools
- Toyota: Shop-floor workers' immediate perception of problems, plus the improvement pressure created by exposing waste

**Diagnostic question:** Remove the subject. Does the energy source still exist? If yes, you've found a real energy, not hero worship.

**Common failure mode:** Attributing success to abstract qualities ("vision," "genius," "leadership") without identifying what concrete energy was being converted.

**Red flag:** If the analysis cannot name at least one specific, external energy source, it has not reached the mechanism layer.

---

## Step 5: Find the Resistance Bypass Mechanism

**Core question:** What did they NOT confront head-on? How did they isolate old resistance and open new space? How did they prevent opponents from assembling?

**Key insight:** The most powerful people are often not the strongest — they are the ones with the least friction. The signature of mastery is not "how they won" but "what they avoided fighting."

**Examples:**
- Deng Xiaoping: Did not directly destroy the old system comprehensively; created incremental space at the margin where old rules had lower resistance
- Grothendieck: Did not attack hard problems directly; built a "rising sea" of new theory where old difficulties naturally softened
- Nintendo (Wii era): Did not compete in the graphics/performance arms race; changed the value axis to novel interaction, making competitors' strengths irrelevant

**Diagnostic questions:**
- What wall did they not hit?
- Where did they open new space?
- How did they make opponents temporarily unable to organize?
- What old rule was simply rendered irrelevant rather than defeated?

**Common failure mode:** Describing victories without analyzing what was NOT fought. If every battle was won head-on, the analysis describes brute force, not strategic genius.

---

## Step 6: Find the Feedback Loop

**Core question:** How did the system know it was wrong? Who bore the cost of errors? How were successes amplified and failures contained locally?

**Why it matters:** A system's sophistication is measured by how it corrects errors, not by how it declares intentions.

**Feedback mechanism categories:**
- Market signals (prices, sales, adoption)
- Competitive pressure
- Peer review / expert criticism
- Performance metrics / experiments / user testing
- Mathematical consistency (for theoretical systems)
- Elections / examinations / audits
- Survival / natural selection (for biological or evolutionary systems)

**Examples:**
- Deng Xiaoping's reform: Local experimentation, success replicated, failure localized; central authority retained final arbitration
- Grothendieck's mathematics: New definitions must continuously return to old problems and verify explanatory power; abstraction that cannot digest old facts is bad abstraction
- Toyota: Andon cord stops the line immediately; problem is visible, immediate, and owned by the person who sees it
- Jobs: User experience testing with real people; if it doesn't feel inevitable, it's wrong

**Diagnostic questions:**
- How did the system detect error?
- Who paid for mistakes?
- How were successes amplified to scale?
- How were failures bounded locally so they couldn't cascade?
- Was the feedback loop tight (fast) or loose (slow)?

**Common failure mode:** Praising "learning" or "iteration" without specifying the actual feedback mechanism. "He learned from experience" is not a feedback loop — it's a description of what you wish existed.

## Step 7: Find the Core Invariants

**Core question:** Across multiple successes in different contexts, what repeating generative pattern emerges?

**This is the most critical step.** The core invariants ARE "the person themselves" — the cognitive algorithm that generates correct actions in any dead-end.

**Method:** List every success case. For each case, extract the mechanism (not the move). Then find what repeats across ALL cases.

**Example — Deng Xiaoping's possible invariants:**
1. Strip ideological labels from the situation
2. Lower the stakes of debate (don't argue theory, test practice)
3. Create incremental space at the margin (not center)
4. Release suppressed desire/energy
5. Allow local first-movers
6. Let facts rewrite theory (not theory dictate facts)
7. Retain central final arbitration power

**Example — Grothendieck's possible invariants:**
1. Suspect the existing language/framework
2. Enlarge the object space (not narrow it)
3. Prioritize relationships over isolated entities
4. Absorb exceptions into the new structure
5. Seek the mother problem (not the specific theorem)
6. Let definitions lead, let theorems follow
7. Let hard problems soften in the new environment

**Format:** List as numbered invariants. Each invariant must be abstract enough to deform across contexts, but concrete enough to be testable.

**Common failure mode:** Listing personality traits ("bold," "visionary," "patient") instead of cognitive operations. Invariants must be verbs — operations the subject repeatedly performs on problems.

---

## Step 8: Counterfactual Transfer Tests

**Core question:** Can the distilled generator deform and function in a completely different domain?

**Method:** Test the generator in at least three alternative domains:
1. **Another country / political system** (for political/economic subjects)
2. **A large company / organization** (for organizational subjects)
3. **A completely different field** (mathematics, AI, education, investing — whatever is most distant from the original context)

**For each domain, ask:**
- Would the generator apply?
- What must deform to fit?
- What would break?
- What new capability would emerge?

**Example:** If Deng Xiaoping's generator were applied to a large tech company:
- "Strip ideological labels" → Strip technology religion labels (microservices, AI, blockchain)
- "Create marginal incremental space" → Create a skunkworks team outside normal process
- "Release suppressed energy" → Find engineers whose ideas are blocked by process
- "Let facts rewrite theory" → Let A/B test results override architectural dogma
- "Retain central arbitration" → CTO retains final call, but doesn't pre-decide

**Common failure mode:** Testing against a domain that's too similar to the original. The test only validates if the distance is real.

---

## Step 9: Five Verification Questions

**Final gate.** A distillation is only considered successful if all five questions can be answered confidently.

### 1. Can it explain the subject's MULTIPLE successes, not just one representative work?
- If the generator only fits one case, it's a story summary, not a distillation.
- Test: List every major success. Does the generator explain each one?

### 2. Can it transfer across scenarios?
- If the generator works only in the original context, it's an answer template, not a generator.
- Test: Apply to a domain the subject never touched. Does it still have generative power?

### 3. Is the energy source clearly identified?
- Without an energy source, the generator is a set of empty values.
- Test: Can you point to a concrete desire, tension, or resource that drives the system?

### 4. Is the resistance handling clearly identified?
- Without resistance handling, the generator is strategic fantasy.
- Test: Can you explain what was NOT confronted and how opposition was neutralized?

### 5. Can it explain how to copy it WRONG?
- If you can't explain the failure modes, you haven't understood deeply enough.
- Test: Can you describe at least three distinct ways people commonly mis-copy this person/system? What do they mistake for the essence?

**If any question cannot be answered, the distillation is incomplete.** Mark those gaps explicitly in the report.

## Common Pitfalls Across All Steps

1. **Hero worship mode:** The analysis becomes praise, not extraction. The subject can do no wrong.
2. **After-the-fact rationalization:** Assuming every outcome was intended. Distinguish between designed mechanism and happy accident.
3. **Over-abstraction:** The invariants become so abstract they fit anyone. "He was good at solving problems" fits everyone and explains nothing.
4. **Under-abstraction:** The invariants are too concrete. "He created Special Economic Zones" only fits Deng Xiaoping's China in 1980.
5. **Confirmation bias:** Only looking for evidence that fits the emerging pattern, ignoring counterexamples.
6. **Single-source analysis:** All evidence comes from one biography or one perspective. Cross-reference is essential.

## The Distillation Quality Spectrum

| Level | Name | Characteristics |
|-------|------|----------------|
| L1 | Moves catalog | Lists actions without system functions |
| L2 | Mechanism map | Identifies functions but no generator |
| L3 | Partial generator | Has invariants but fails transfer tests |
| L4 | Validated generator | Passes all five verification questions, transfers across domains |
| L5 | Teachable generator | L4 + clear mis-copying patterns + someone else can apply it |
