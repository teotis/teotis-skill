# Analyze Success: Distilling the Generator from Any Success

## 0. Mission and Boundaries

### Core Mission

> **Distill not "what they did" (answer templates), but "how they generated correct actions in unfamiliar dead-end situations" (the problem-solving engine / cognitive algorithm).**

The central distinction is between copying answers and understanding the engine that produces them. A method distilled from answers — "he was pragmatic," "he made great products" — fails the moment context changes. A generator distilled from the engine deforms across contexts and keeps producing valid moves.

The purpose is not to produce a biography, a timeline, or a list of achievements. It is to extract the **generative cognitive algorithm** — what this person or system reliably does when facing a completely novel dead-end — so that the reader can internalize the algorithm, not just its historical outputs.

### Boundaries

1. **Read-only analysis.** This skill does not modify code, data, configuration, or production systems. It produces an HTML analysis report.
2. **Subject agnostic.** Analyze any success: individuals (Deng Xiaoping, Jobs, Grothendieck), organizations (Toyota, Nintendo, DARPA), products (iPhone, AlphaGo, ChatGPT), strategies (Blue Ocean, disruptive innovation), or systems (markets, constitutions, scientific paradigms).
3. **Minimum evidence threshold.** A single success is insufficient for generator-level claims. When evidence falls below the threshold (fewer than 3 cross-context successes), flag the limitation explicitly and limit the analysis to moves and possible mechanisms with strong caveats.
4. **Fact/inference separation.** Every claim must be tagged as Documented Fact, Pattern Inference, or Speculative Hypothesis. The Verification Ledger makes this separation visible.
5. **No hero worship.** The report must treat the subject as an analyzable system, not a legend. "Genius" is not an explanation — it's a placeholder for an unexplained mechanism.

## 1. Invocation Flow

When this skill is invoked:

1. If no subject is provided, ask: **"Who or what do you want to analyze? Provide a specific person, organization, product, or system."**
2. For living contemporaries or subjects with limited public evidence, warn about evidence constraints and adjust confidence accordingly.
3. Assess cross-context repeatability. If fewer than 3 distinct successes in different contexts exist, flag the limitation and proceed only with moves and possible mechanisms, not generator-level claims.
4. Gather documented evidence:
   - Critical dead-end situations faced and decisions made
   - Outcomes across multiple contexts and time periods
   - Behavioral patterns, recurring themes, documented reasoning
   - What contemporaries did differently in similar situations
   - Energy sources: desires, tensions, resources tapped
   - Resistance: what was avoided, bypassed, or rendered irrelevant
   - Feedback mechanisms: how the system detected and corrected errors
5. Build a **Verification Ledger** separating Documented Fact, Pattern Inference, and Speculative Hypothesis.
6. Apply the nine-step distillation method sequentially. Each step feeds the next.
7. Run counterfactual transfer tests in at least 3 alternative domains.
8. Validate with the five verification questions.
9. Generate the interactive HTML report (`analyze_success_report.html`).
10. Open the report in a browser when the environment supports it. If automatic launch fails, state the file path and reason honestly.

## 2. The Three-Layer Analysis Model

Every success can be examined at three layers. The distinction between them is the core of this skill.

### Layer 1: Moves (招式)

**What they are:** Observable, surface-level actions, policies, decisions, products, or pronouncements.

**Characteristics:** Easiest to see. Easiest to copy. Easiest to copy wrong. Highly dependent on historical context.

**Examples:**
- Deng Xiaoping: Special Economic Zones, reform and opening-up, "black cat white cat," household responsibility system
- Grothendieck: schemes, toposes, EGA, SGA, "rising sea"
- Steve Jobs: minimal design, product keynotes, integrated hardware-software
- Toyota: kanban, lean production, andon cord, just-in-time

**Why copying moves fails:** You can copy Special Economic Zones without getting Shenzhen. You can copy minimal design without getting Apple. You can copy scheme language without getting structural insight. Moves are context-bound outputs of a deeper engine — copying the output reproduces neither the engine nor the context.

### Layer 2: Mechanisms (机制)

**What they are:** The system function each move served. What role did the move play in the larger system?

**Mandatory translation formula:**

> **X is not X; its function is Y.**

**Examples:**
- "Special Economic Zones are not preferential zones; their function is institutional experiment sandboxes with low resistance from old rules."
- "Pragmatism is not a personality trait; its function is switching the evaluation criterion from ideological purity to performance feedback."
- "Schemes are not abstract nouns; their function is enlarging the definition of 'space' so that number theory, geometry, local rings, degenerations, and singular phenomena enter the same language."
- "Kanban is not sticky notes; its function is making inventory waste visible and compressing feedback to the shop floor."
- "Abstraction is not distance from reality; its function is finding higher-dimensional operational space."
- "Brand is not a logo; its function is compressing trust and aesthetic judgment."

**Why mechanism-level analysis is better:** Understanding functions allows you to invent new moves when context changes. But it still has a limitation — mechanisms explain one move at a time, while the generator explains where all the mechanisms come from.

### Layer 3: Generator (生成器)

**What it is:** The cognitive algorithm that, when facing a completely novel dead-end, reliably produces:
1. A redefinition of the problem
2. Identification of usable energy
3. A path around resistance
4. A feedback mechanism for course correction
5. Moves that embody all of the above

**This is the essence.** It is not what the subject did in situation A, B, or C. It is what they will do in situation Z, which has never existed before.

**Examples (hypothetical distillations):**
- Deng Xiaoping's generator: Strip ideological labels → identify suppressed real energy → create low-risk incremental space at the margin → let local experiments run → use results to rewrite theory → retain final arbitration.
- Grothendieck's generator: Suspect the existing language → enlarge the conceptual space → absorb exceptions into a new natural structure → let hard problems soften in the expanded environment → validate by digesting old facts.
- Jobs' generator: Start from the complete user experience → reverse-engineer technology, organization, and supply chain from that endpoint → refuse to compromise the experience axis → make alternatives feel broken.

**The entire purpose of this skill is to reach Layer 3.** If a report stops at Layers 1 or 2, it has not completed its mission.

## 3. The Nine-Step Distillation Method

Each step produces specific outputs. Steps must be applied sequentially — each feeds the next. Detailed methodology with examples and failure modes is in `references/nine_step_framework.md`.

### Step 1: Find Multiple Successes

**Question:** Is the success pattern repeated across contexts, time periods, and problem domains?

**Requirement:** At least 3 distinct successes in different contexts. If N < 3, limit analysis to moves and possible mechanisms.

**Output:** A list of verified successes with context, period, and nature of the challenge.

### Step 2: Recover the Original Problem

**Question:** What dead-end did they face that others could not see clearly? How did they redefine the problem differently?

**Formula:** Others saw the problem as **[X]**. The subject redefined it as **[Y]**.

**Output:** The original problem statement and its redefinition, with evidence.

### Step 3: Translate Actions into System Functions

**Question:** For each significant move, what system function did it serve?

**Formula:** X is not X; its function is Y.

**Output:** A translated list mapping each observable move to its mechanism function.

### Step 4: Find the Energy Converter

**Question:** What concrete desire, resource, contradiction, fear, competition, or tension was converted into constructive driving force?

**Output:** Named energy source(s) and the conversion mechanism.

### Step 5: Find the Resistance Bypass Mechanism

**Question:** What was NOT confronted head-on? How was old resistance isolated? Where was new space opened?

**Output:** The specific bypass path, the isolated resistance, and the new operational space created.

### Step 6: Find the Feedback Loop

**Question:** How did the system know it was wrong? Who bore the cost? How were successes amplified and failures localized?

**Output:** The feedback mechanism type, its speed (tight or loose), and its error-containment property.

### Step 7: Find the Core Invariants

**Question:** Across all examined successes, what repeating generative pattern emerges?

**This is the most critical step.** The invariants ARE "the person themselves."

**Output:** A numbered list of cognitive operations that repeat across all cases. Each invariant must be a verb — an operation the subject performs on problems.

### Step 8: Counterfactual Transfer Tests

**Question:** Can the distilled generator deform and function in at least 3 completely different domains?

**Domains:** Another country, a large company, a completely different field (mathematics, AI, education, investing).

**Output:** For each domain: applicability, required deformation, what breaks, new capability.

### Step 9: Five Verification Questions

**The final quality gate.** A distillation passes only when all five questions are answerable:

1. **Multi-success coverage:** Does it explain multiple successes, not just one?
2. **Cross-scenario transfer:** Can it deform across domains?
3. **Energy clarity:** Is the energy source named and verifiable?
4. **Resistance clarity:** Is the bypass mechanism specific?
5. **Wrong-copy diagnosis:** Can you describe at least 3 distinct ways people mis-copy this?

**If any question cannot be answered, the distillation is incomplete.** Mark gaps in the report.

## 4. Analysis Rhythm

### Phase 0: Define Subject and Scope
- Record the subject and the user's specific interest or question.
- Identify the domain (politics, business, science, art, technology, etc.).
- Note evidence availability and any known limitations.

### Phase 1: Gather Evidence and Build Verification Ledger
- Collect documented decisions, outcomes, dead-end situations, behavioral patterns.
- Build and populate the Verification Ledger.

### Phase 2: Apply the Nine-Step Method
- Execute steps 1–7 sequentially.
- Each step produces outputs that feed the next step.
- Do not skip steps. Do not jump to the generator before completing the mechanism layer.
- Mark gaps where evidence is insufficient.

### Phase 3: Run Counterfactual and Falsification Tests
- Apply step 8 (transfer tests) in at least 3 domains.
- Actively seek counterexamples: times when the subject failed or deviated from the pattern.
- Test whether the generator explains the failures as well as the successes.

### Phase 4: Final Validation
- Apply step 9 (five verification questions).
- Classify the distillation quality on the L1–L5 spectrum.
- Identify the most likely error or blind spot in the current analysis.

### Phase 5: Generate Report
- Produce `analyze_success_report.html` with all required sections.
- Open in browser when feasible.

## 5. Verification Ledger Format

Every claim in the report must be traceable to one of these categories:

| Category | Meaning | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| Documented Fact | Supported by multiple independent sources, direct quotes, or verifiable outcomes | "Deng established four SEZs in 1980" |
| Pattern Inference | Reasonable conclusion from multiple consistent observations, but not directly stated by the subject | "The SEZs were designed to contain risk rather than maximize immediate output" |
| Speculative Hypothesis | Plausible but lacks sufficient evidence; marked for further validation | "If Deng faced today's tech landscape, his generator would likely target regulatory sandboxes" |

**Rule:** Never present inference as fact. Never present hypothesis as inference.

**Ledger table format:**

| ID | Claim | Category | Evidence Source | Confidence | Unknown |
|----|-------|----------|----------------|------------|---------|
| F-01 | ... | Documented Fact | ... | High | — |
| I-01 | ... | Pattern Inference | ... | Medium | ... |
| H-01 | ... | Speculative Hypothesis | ... | Low | ... |

## 6. HTML Report Specification

### Report Objective

The report must allow the reader to answer:

- What is the generator — the cognitive algorithm that produces success?
- What energy does it convert, and what resistance does it bypass?
- What are the specific mechanisms, not just the surface moves?
- What are the core invariants that repeat across all successes?
- Can the generator transfer to completely different domains?
- How do people reliably mis-copy this, and why?
- What is the strongest counterargument or evidence gap?

Default filename: `analyze_success_report.html`.

Where browser launching is possible, automatically open the report after generation:
- macOS: `open -a "Google Chrome" <path>`
- Linux: `google-chrome <path>`
- Windows: `start chrome <path>`

If automatic opening fails, provide the absolute file path and state why.

### Visual Design

- Default dark theme with background `#15191f`. Avoid pure black and pure white.
- Left-side sticky table of contents with `IntersectionObserver` scroll-linked highlighting.
- Present findings through cards, badges, and grouped tables.
- Expandable `<details>` sections for detailed evidence and methodology.

**Badge semantics:**

| Badge | Color | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Generator | `#d4a853` (gold) | Core invariant / cognitive algorithm found |
| Mechanism | `#58a6ff` (blue) | System function identified |
| Move | `#8b949e` (gray) | Surface action |
| Verified | `#3fb950` (green) | Evidence confirmed |
| Hypothesis | `#d29922` (yellow) | Plausible but unverified |
| Wrong Copy | `#f85149` (red) | Common mis-copying pattern |
| Falsified | `#f85149` (red) | Tested and disproven |

### Mandatory Report Sections

#### 0. Cover and One-Paragraph Essence
- Subject name, domain, analysis date.
- One-paragraph distillation of the generator essence.
- Evidence completeness statement.
- Explicit: this is an analytical report, not a biography.

#### 1. Executive Summary
- The generator kernel (3–5 sentence distillation).
- The most important mechanism (the key conversion).
- What NOT to copy (most common error).
- Distillation quality level (L1–L5) with confidence.

#### 2. Three-Layer Model Visualization
- Interactive Mermaid diagram: Moves (bottom, gray) → Mechanisms (middle, blue) → Generator (top, gold).
- Each node clickable to scroll to its detail section.
- The diagram must make the layer distinctions immediately visible.

#### 3. Multi-Success Evidence Board
- Table of verified successes with context, period, challenge type, and outcome.
- Cross-context repeatability assessment.
- If N < 3, explicit warning and limited scope statement.

#### 4. Nine-Step Distillation
Each step gets its own card containing:
- Step number and diagnostic question.
- Key findings.
- Evidence quality badge.
- The specific outputs required by the step.

#### 5. Core Invariants (The Generator)
This is the largest section. Present:
- Numbered list of cognitive operations (the invariants).
- Each invariant accompanied by evidence traces from multiple contexts.
- Each invariant tested against the "is it a verb" criterion.
- Mermaid diagram: Energy and Resistance Flow — energy source → conversion → output; resistance → bypass → new space; feedback loop at bottom.

#### 6. Counterfactual Transfer Tests
- CSS grid or table showing the generator tested in at least 3 alternative domains.
- Columns: Domain, Applicable?, Must Deform, Would Break, Confidence.
- Specific, concrete scenarios — not generic assertions.

#### 7. Verification and Falsification
- Five verification questions answered individually.
- Each with confidence rating and evidence.
- Known counterexamples or failures explicitly discussed.
- What would falsify the current distillation.

#### 8. Common Mis-Copying Patterns
- At least 3 distinct ways people reliably misunderstand and mis-apply this subject.
- Each pattern: what people think is the essence vs. what the actual mechanism is.
- Why the mis-copy version fails.

#### 9. Limitations and Missing Evidence
- Evidence gaps and what additional information would change the analysis.
- Alternative interpretations not ruled out.
- Most likely error in the current distillation.
- Confidence level and the next best investigation.

## 8. Report Voice and Output Standard

### The Report Should Read Like
- A rigorous analysis by someone who can think independently about the subject.
- Evidence-anchored: every claim traces to a documented fact, inference, or hypothesis.
- Layered: the reader can stop at moves, go deeper to mechanisms, or reach the generator.
- Honest about uncertainty: gaps, counterexamples, and weak evidence are surfaced, not buried.
- Practical: the reader should finish knowing what to internalize and what to avoid copying.

### The Report Must Not Read Like
- A biography or chronological summary.
- Hero worship or hagiography.
- A collection of quotes.
- An academic paper that obscures insight behind methodology.
- A generic self-help article ("5 lessons from X").
- A document that claims certainty where evidence is thin.

## 9. Anti-Patterns and Quality Safeguards

### Do NOT Produce

1. **Moves-only catalog:** A list of what the subject did with no mechanism or generator analysis.
2. **Single-source hero worship:** Analysis based on one biography or perspective, presenting the subject as flawless.
3. **Generator from thin evidence:** Confident generator claims from a single success or insufficient data.
4. **Over-abstraction:** Invariants so generic they fit anyone ("he was good at solving problems").
5. **Under-abstraction:** Invariants too concrete to transfer ("he created Special Economic Zones").
6. **Missing energy source:** Generator claims without identifying what concrete energy was converted.
7. **Missing resistance analysis:** Generator claims without explaining what was bypassed or avoided.
8. **No wrong-copy diagnosis:** A report that cannot explain how people reliably misunderstand the subject.

### Quality Self-Check

Before delivering, verify:

1. Does the report distinguish between at least 3 moves, 3 mechanisms, and 1 generator?
2. Is the "X is not X; its function is Y" formula used at least 3 times?
3. Is the energy source named specifically (not "vision" or "genius")?
4. Is the resistance bypass mechanism concrete (not "he overcame obstacles")?
5. Are there at least 3 counterfactual transfer domains tested?
6. Are all 5 verification questions answered?
7. Are there at least 3 wrong-copy patterns described?
8. Is every claim tagged with a Verification Ledger category?
